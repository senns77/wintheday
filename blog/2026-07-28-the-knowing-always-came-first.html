The Knowing Always Came First

Before the Wayne Gretzky draw, before the $550K deals, before every major win—there was a quiet certainty that arrived without evidence. That pattern changed everything.

← Back to the dispatch

I've been looking back at every major thing I've ever built or won, and there's a pattern I can't ignore anymore.

The knowing always came first.

Not the plan. Not the strategy. Not the evidence. The knowing.

I was ten years old when my name got drawn to meet Wayne Gretzky. But before they pulled the slip of paper, before anyone said my name out loud, I already knew. It wasn't hope. It wasn't wishful thinking. It was a calm, sourceless certainty that landed in my chest like something I'd already lived through.

Late twenties, working in sales. I lost a big deal mid-month and the pipeline was thin. I needed that commission to pay my mortgage. But I knew—not hoped, knew—I would close something by month end. I did.

July 2022. The company was in a financial crunch. Pressure was mounting from every direction. I had no deals in motion, no logical reason to feel calm. But I knew I would find a large deal before the month ended. I found two. They totaled over $550K. The knowing came first. The evidence followed.

This isn't about manifestation in the way most people talk about it. I'm not saying I visualized hard enough or repeated affirmations until I believed them. I'm saying there was a state of being that preceded the outcome. A frequency. A certainty so quiet it didn't need to defend itself.

David Cameron Gikandi talks about this in a way that finally made sense to me: the universe doesn't respond to what you know intellectually. It responds to what you are vibrationally. The knowing isn't a thought. It's a state. And when you're in that state, the external world reorganizes itself around it.

The Difference Between Hoping and Knowing

Hope is future tense. It lives in the gap between where you are and where you want to be. It's exhausting because it requires constant maintenance. You have to keep feeding it, protecting it, defending it against doubt.

The knowing is present tense. It doesn't live in the gap. It collapses the gap. It's the experience of already being the person who has what you're building toward. Not performing it. Not faking it. Actually inhabiting it.

When I look at the moments that mattered most—the wins that changed everything—they all started the same way. A quiet arrival. A certainty with no origin story. A sense that the thing I was reaching for was already mine, and I was just walking toward the evidence of it.

I write about this more in the Win The Day dispatch—the difference between building from scarcity and building from the knowing. It's the thread that runs through everything.

The question I'm sitting with now is this: what if the knowing isn't rare? What if it's always available, and we've just been trained to ignore it in favor of logic, proof, and the appearance of being reasonable?

What if the knowing is the signal, and everything else is just noise?
